## 3. Project Title

**Course Registration System**

A system that lets a university manage its course offerings, student enrollment, and registration reports in one place.

## 4. Problem Statement

At the start of every semester, course registration puts heavy pressure on a university's administrative staff. When enrollment is handled through paper forms, spreadsheets, or separate departmental records, every request has to be checked by hand: whether the student has passed the prerequisites, whether the section still has free seats, and whether the new course overlaps with the student's timetable. A missed check leaves a student double-booked or a classroom overfilled, and the correction often comes weeks later.

Because each department keeps its own records, enrollment figures are rarely current. Managers cannot see which sections are full and which are nearly empty until the registration period has already closed. The university needs one centralized system that applies the registration rules consistently, keeps a single reliable record of enrollment, and gives staff and managers up-to-date information when they need it.

## 5. Project Objectives

The project is guided by the following objectives.

1. Analyze the requirements of a course registration system, including its users, main processes, and business rules (prerequisites, seat limits, and schedule conflicts).
2. Design the system models using BPM, Use Case, and ERD diagrams.
3. Document the requirements and models clearly enough for a development team to use them.
4. Understand the importance of system analysis before development.

## 6. Scope of the System

The proposed system covers four functional areas, described below.

| Functional area | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| User registration and authentication | Accounts and role-based access for students, staff, managers, and administrators |
| Course and section management | Course catalog, prerequisites, sections, instructors, class schedules, and seat capacity |
| Registration processing and tracking | Enrolling in and dropping courses, prerequisite and timetable-conflict checks, waitlists, and registration status |
| Enrollment and performance reporting | Class lists, seat utilization, and enrollment statistics by course and department |

**Out of scope:** tuition payment, grading and transcripts, and software implementation. This project covers system analysis and design only.

## 7. Expected Users

The system is intended for four groups of users.

| User group | Role and main activities |
| --- | --- |
| Student | Searches the course catalog, registers for and drops courses, and checks their timetable and registration status |
| Staff / Employee (registrar office staff and instructors) | Maintains course and section data, handles special requests, and views class lists |
| Manager (department head) | Monitors enrollment and seat utilization and decides on changes to course offerings |
| System Administrator | Manages user accounts, access rights, and system settings |

## 9. Expected Outcomes

By the end of the project, the team expects to gain practical experience in system analysis and design through a realistic case, a better understanding of UML and database modeling, and the ability to document system requirements clearly.

The project will also produce a complete set of deliverables: a requirements list, BPM diagrams, Use Case diagrams with descriptions, an ERD with a data dictionary, and the project presentation.

## 10. Conclusion

Course registration is a high-volume process in which one overlooked rule, such as a missed prerequisite or an overfilled section, affects students, instructors, and administrators at the same time. By analyzing the process and modeling it with BPM, Use Case, and ERD diagrams before any development begins, the team can identify these rules early and define them clearly. The resulting models will give the proposed system a reliable foundation and show how careful analysis and design improve efficiency, accuracy, and management control.
